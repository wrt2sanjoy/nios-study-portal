MODULE 1: MEASUREMENT IN SCIENCE

Lesson 1: Measurement in Science & Technology

  • Fundamental Quantities & SI Units: Length ($\text{m}$), Mass ($\text{kg}$), Time ($\text{s}$), Temperature ($\text{K}$), Electric Current ($\text{A}$), Luminous Intensity ($\text{cd}$), Amount of Substance ($\text{mol}$).
  • Derived Quantities: Area ($\text{m}^2$), Volume ($\text{m}^3$), Density ($\text{kg/m}^3$), Speed ($\text{m/s}$), Force ($\text{N}$).
  • Key Instruments: Vernier Caliper (least count $0.01\text{ cm} = 0.1\text{ mm}$), Screw Gauge / Micrometer (least count $0.01\text{ mm}$).

MODULE 2: MATTER IN OUR SURROUNDINGS (CHEMISTRY)

Lesson 5: Atomic Structure

  • Subatomic Particles: Electron ($-1$, Thomson), Proton ($+1$, Rutherford), Neutron ($0$, Chadwick).
  • Bohr's Model: Electrons revolve in discrete circular orbits (energy shells $K, L, M, N$ with maximum $2n^2$ electrons: $2, 8, 18, 32$).
  • Atomic Number ($Z$) = Number of protons.
  • Mass Number ($A$) = Protons + Neutrons.
  • Isotopes vs Isobars:
  • Isotopes: Same atomic number $Z$, different mass number $A$ (e.g. $^{12}_6\text{C}$ and $^{14}_6\text{C}$; $^{1}_1\text{H}$, $^{2}_1\text{H}$, $^{3}_1\text{H}$).
  • Isobars: Same mass number $A$, different atomic number $Z$ (e.g. $^{40}_{18}\text{Ar}$ and $^{40}_{20}\text{Ca}$).

Lesson 6: Periodic Classification of Elements

  • Modern Periodic Law: Physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.
  • Modern Periodic Table Layout: 18 vertical columns (Groups) and 7 horizontal rows (Periods).
  • Periodic Trends:
  • Valency: Across period: increases from 1 to 4, then decreases to 0. Down a group: remains constant.
  • Atomic Size (Radius): Decreases left to right across a period (due to increasing nuclear charge pulling electrons closer); Increases down a group (due to addition of new electron shells).
  • Metallic Character: Decreases across a period; increases down a group.

Lesson 7: Chemical Bonding

  • Octet Rule: Atoms gain, lose, or share valence electrons to attain a stable noble gas configuration (8 electrons in outermost shell).
  • Ionic (Electrovalent) Bond: Formed by complete transfer of electrons from a metal to a non-metal (e.g., $\text{NaCl}, \text{MgCl}_2$). High melting points, soluble in water, conduct electricity in molten/aqueous state.
  • Covalent Bond: Formed by mutual sharing of electron pairs between non-metals (e.g., $\text{H}_2, \text{O}_2, \text{CH}_4, \text{H}_2\text{O}$). Lower melting points, non-conductors of electricity.

Lesson 8: Acids, Bases and Salts

  • Acids: Taste sour, produce $\text{H}^+$ ions in water, turn blue litmus red.
  • Bases: Taste bitter, soapy to touch, produce $\text{OH}^-$ ions in water, turn red litmus blue.
  • Salts: Formed by neutralization: $\text{Acid} + \text{Base} \to \text{Salt} + \text{Water}$.
  • Master Equations:
  • Bleaching Powder: $\text{Ca(OH)}_2 + \text{Cl}_2 \to \text{CaOCl}_2 + \text{H}_2\text{O}$
  • Baking Soda: $\text{NaCl} + \text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{CO}_2 + \text{NH}_3 \to \text{NH}_4\text{Cl} + \text{NaHCO}_3$
  • POP: $\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \xrightarrow{373\text{ K}} \text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O} + 1.5\text{H}_2\text{O}$

MODULE 3: MOVING THINGS (PHYSICS)

Lesson 10: Force and Motion

  • Newton's First Law: A body continues in state of rest or uniform motion unless acted upon by an external unbalanced force (Law of Inertia).
  • Newton's Second Law: Rate of change of momentum is directly proportional to applied force: $F = ma$.
  • Newton's Third Law: To every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction acting on two different bodies.
  • Law of Conservation of Momentum: In an isolated system, total momentum before collision equals total momentum after collision: $m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2$.

Lesson 11: Gravitation

  • Universal Law of Gravitation: $F = G \frac{M m}{d^2}$ ($G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$).
  • Acceleration due to Gravity ($g$): $g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2$ on Earth; $g_{\text{moon}} = \frac{1}{6} g_{\text{earth}}$.
  • Difference between Mass and Weight:
  • Mass: Quantity of matter; constant everywhere; scalar; measured in $\text{kg}$.
  • Weight: Gravitational force $W = mg$; varies with location; vector; measured in $\text{N}$.
  • Archimedes' Principle: An immersed body experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced.

MODULE 4: ENERGY (PHYSICS)

Lesson 13: Work and Energy

  • Work: $W = F \cdot s$ (Joules). No work is done if force is perpendicular to displacement ($\cos 90^\circ = 0$).
  • Kinetic Energy: $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.
  • Potential Energy: $PE = mgh$.
  • Law of Conservation of Energy: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; only converted from one form to another. Total mechanical energy $E = KE + PE$ remains constant.
  • Power: $P = \frac{W}{t}$ (Watts). $1\text{ kW} = 1000\text{ W}$.

Lesson 15: Light Energy

  • Reflection: Angle of incidence $=$ Angle of reflection ($\angle i = \angle r$).
  • Mirror Formula: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$, Magnification $m = -\frac{v}{u}$.
  • Refraction & Snell's Law: $\mu = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r}$. Light bends towards normal entering a denser medium.
  • Lens Formula: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}$, Magnification $m = +\frac{v}{u}$.
  • Power of Lens: $P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (m)}}$ (Dioptres). Convex is $+$, Concave is $-$.
  • Eye Defects: Myopia (corrected by concave lens) vs Hypermetropia (corrected by convex lens).

Lesson 16: Electrical Energy

  • Ohm's Law: $V = IR$.
  • Resistance Factors: $R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$.
  • Resistors: Series $R_s = R_1 + R_2$; Parallel $\frac{1}{R_p} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}$.
  • Joule's Heating: $H = I^2 Rt$.
  • Commercial Energy: $1\text{ kWh} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J} = 1\text{ Unit}$.

Lesson 17: Magnetic Effect of Electric Current

  • Right-Hand Thumb Rule: Thumb indicates current direction; curled fingers indicate circular magnetic field lines.
  • Solenoid: Coil of insulated wire; magnetic field inside a long current-carrying solenoid is uniform and strong (behaves like a bar magnet).
  • Fleming's Left-Hand Rule: Used in electric motors (Thumb = Motion, Index = Field, Middle = Current).
  • Safety Devices: Earth wire (diverts leakage current safely to ground), Fuse (melts and breaks circuit during overload/short-circuit).

MODULE 5: THE LIVING WORLD (BIOLOGY)

Lesson 22: Life Processes – I (Nutrition, Respiration, Circulation, Excretion)

  • Photosynthesis: Conversion of solar energy to chemical energy in green plants using chlorophyll.
  • Human Digestion: Salivary amylase in mouth, Pepsin & $\text{HCl}$ in stomach, Bile & Pancreatic juice (trypsin, lipase) in small intestine, absorption through villi.
  • Respiration: Breakdown of glucose: Aerobic (mitochondria, 38 ATP) vs Anaerobic (yeast: ethanol; muscle: lactic acid, 2 ATP).
  • Circulation: 4-chambered human heart prevents mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood; Double circulation.
  • Excretion: Nephron filters blood in kidneys; 3 steps: Ultrafiltration $\to$ Selective Reabsorption $\to$ Tubular Secretion.

Lesson 23: Life Processes – II (Control & Coordination)

  • Neuron Structure: Dendrite $\to$ Cyton (cell body) $\to$ Axon $\to$ Nerve ending $\to$ Synapse (chemical transmission via neurotransmitter).
  • Reflex Arc: Receptor $\to$ Sensory neuron $\to$ Spinal cord $\to$ Motor neuron $\to$ Effector muscle.
  • Plant Hormones (Phytohormones):
  • Auxin: Shoot elongation and phototropism.
  • Gibberellin: Stem growth.
  • Cytokinin: Promotes cell division.
  • Abscisic Acid (ABA): Growth inhibitor, wilting of leaves.
  • Endocrine Glands: Pancreas (Insulin), Thyroid (Thyroxine requires iodine), Adrenal (Adrenaline - fight/flight hormone).

Lesson 24: Life Processes – III (Reproduction)

  • Asexual Reproduction: Binary fission (Amoeba), Budding (Yeast, Hydra), Spore formation (Rhizopus), Fragmentation (Spirogyra), Vegetative propagation (Potato, Rose).
  • Sexual Reproduction in Plants: Flower parts (Stamen = male: anther + filament; Carpel = female: stigma + style + ovary). Pollination (self/cross) followed by fertilization.
  • Human Reproduction: Testes produce sperm and testosterone; Ovaries produce ova, estrogen, and progesterone. Fertilization occurs in Fallopian tube.

Lesson 25: Heredity

  • Mendel's Laws: Law of Segregation and Law of Independent Assortment.
  • Monohybrid Cross: Phenotypic ratio $3 : 1$, Genotypic ratio $1 : 2 : 1$.
  • Sex Determination in Humans: $44 + XX$ (female) and $44 + XY$ (male). Father determines child's sex.

MODULE 6: NATURAL RESOURCES (CHEMISTRY)

Lesson 27: Metals and Non-metals

  • Reactivity Series: $\text{K} > \text{Na} > \text{Ca} > \dots > \text{Cu} > \text{Ag} > \text{Au}$.
  • Extraction of Metals:
  • Sulphide ores $\to$ Roasting (excess air).
  • Carbonate ores $\to$ Calcination (limited air).
  • Corrosion: Oxidation of metals exposed to moisture and air. Prevention: Galvanization (coating iron with zinc), painting, greasing, making alloys (e.g. Stainless steel: $\text{Fe} + \text{Cr} + \text{Ni}$).

Lesson 28: Carbon and Its Compounds

  • Catenation & Tetravalency: Give rise to huge variety of carbon compounds.
  • Homologous Series: Family of organic compounds having same functional group and general formula; successive members differ by $-\text{CH}_2-$ unit and $14\text{ u}$ molecular mass.
  • Soaps and Detergents: Soaps form micelles to remove grease; ineffective in hard water due to scum formation ($\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{Mg}^{2+}$ precipitates). Synthetic detergents work effectively even in hard water.

MODULE 7: HUMAN AND ENVIRONMENT (ECOLOGY)

Lesson 29: Natural Environment

  • Ecosystem: Functional unit comprising biotic components (plants, animals, decomposers) and abiotic components (air, water, soil, sunlight).
  • Food Chain & Food Web: Linear flow of energy vs interconnected network of food chains.
  • 10% Law of Energy: Only 10% of energy transfers to the next trophic level; limits food chains to 3–4 trophic levels.

Lesson 30: Human Impact on Environment

  • Ozone Layer Depletion: $\text{O}_3$ layer in stratosphere shields Earth from UV rays. Depleted by CFCs from cooling appliances.
  • Biomagnification: Progressive accumulation of toxic, non-biodegradable chemicals (like DDT) along food chains.
  • Waste Management: Segregation into Biodegradable (organic, decomposes naturally) and Non-biodegradable (plastics, glass; requires 3Rs: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle).
