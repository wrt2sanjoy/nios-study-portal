Template 1: Heights and Distances (5 Marks — Q44)

Problem (Real Exam Question from 2024 & 2025 Papers):

As observed from the top of a $100\text{ m}$ high lighthouse from the sea level, the angles of depression of two ships on the opposite sides of the lighthouse are $60^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ respectively. Find the distance between the two ships. (Take $\sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$).

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Draw the Diagram:
  2. Let $AB$ be the lighthouse of height $h = 100\text{ m}$.
  3. Let $C$ and $D$ be the two ships on opposite sides on the sea level along a straight line.
  4. The foot of the lighthouse is $B$.
  5. Angle of elevation from ship $C$ to top $A$ is $45^\circ$ (alternate interior angle to angle of depression).
  6. Angle of elevation from ship $D$ to top $A$ is $60^\circ$.
                  A (Top of Lighthouse, 100m)
                 /| \
                / |  \
               /  |   \
              /   |    \
             /    |     \
   (Ship 1) C-----B------D (Ship 2)
           <--x--><--y--->

  1. In right $\Delta ABC$: $$\tan 45^\circ = \frac{AB}{BC}$$ Since $\tan 45^\circ = 1$ and $AB = 100$: $$1 = \frac{100}{BC} \implies BC = 100\text{ m}$$

  2. In right $\Delta ABD$: $$\tan 60^\circ = \frac{AB}{BD}$$ Since $\tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}$ and $AB = 100$: $$\sqrt{3} = \frac{100}{BD} \implies BD = \frac{100}{\sqrt{3}}$$ Rationalize the denominator: $$BD = \frac{100\sqrt{3}}{3} = \frac{100 \times 1.732}{3} = \frac{173.2}{3} \approx 57.73\text{ m}$$

  3. Total distance between the ships: $$\text{Distance } CD = BC + BD = 100 + 57.73 = \mathbf{157.73\text{ m}}$$

(Note: If ships were on the SAME side of the lighthouse, distance would be $BC - BD = 100 - 57.73 = 42.27\text{ m}$)

Template 2: Graphical Solution of Linear Equations (5 Marks — Q43)

Problem (Real Exam Question):

Solve the following system of linear equations graphically: $$2x + y = 6$$ $$2x - y = -2 \quad (\text{or } 2x - y + 2 = 0)$$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Table of Values for Equation 1: $2x + y = 6 \implies y = 6 - 2x$
  2. If $x = 0 \implies y = 6 - 2(0) = 6 \implies (0, 6)$
  3. If $x = 2 \implies y = 6 - 2(2) = 2 \implies (2, 2)$
  4. If $x = 3 \implies y = 6 - 2(3) = 0 \implies (3, 0)$
$x$ $0$ $2$ $3$
$y$ $6$ $2$ $0$
  1. Table of Values for Equation 2: $2x - y = -2 \implies y = 2x + 2$
  2. If $x = 0 \implies y = 2(0) + 2 = 2 \implies (0, 2)$
  3. If $x = 1 \implies y = 2(1) + 2 = 4 \implies (1, 4)$
  4. If $x = -1 \implies y = 2(-1) + 2 = 0 \implies (-1, 0)$
$x$ $0$ $1$ $-1$
$y$ $2$ $4$ $0$
  1. Plotting on Graph Sheet:
  2. Plot points $(0, 6), (2, 2), (3, 0)$ and draw a straight line through them, labeling it $2x + y = 6$.

  3. Plot points $(0, 2), (1, 4), (-1, 0)$ and draw a straight line through them, labeling it $2x - y = -2$.

  4. Point of Intersection:

  5. Both lines intersect at the point $P(1, 4)$.
  6. Conclusion: The solution to the system is $\mathbf{x = 1, \; y = 4}$. (Quick check: $2(1) + 4 = 6$ ✓, $2(1) - 4 = -2$ ✓)

Template 3: Construction of Tangents to a Circle (3 to 5 Marks — Q39/Q43)

Problem:

Draw a circle of radius $3\text{ cm}$. From a point $P$ at a distance of $7\text{ cm}$ from the centre $O$, construct a pair of tangents to the circle. Measure the length of the tangents.

Step-by-Step Construction Steps (Write these exact steps in your answer sheet):

  1. Step 1: Mark a point $O$ as the centre and draw a circle of radius $3\text{ cm}$ using a compass.
  2. Step 2: Take a point $P$ such that $OP = 7\text{ cm}$. Join $OP$.
  3. Step 3: Draw the perpendicular bisector of the line segment $OP$. Let $M$ be the mid-point of $OP$ ($OM = MP = 3.5\text{ cm}$).
  4. Step 4: With $M$ as centre and radius $MO$ (or $MP$), draw a circle that cuts the given circle at two points, say $T_1$ and $T_2$.
  5. Step 5: Join $PT_1$ and $PT_2$.
  6. $PT_1$ and $PT_2$ are the required pair of tangents from point $P$ to the circle.

Mathematical Verification:

In right $\Delta OT_1 P$ ($\angle OT_1 P = 90^\circ$ because tangent $\perp$ radius): $$PT_1 = \sqrt{OP^2 - OT_1^2} = \sqrt{7^2 - 3^2} = \sqrt{49 - 9} = \sqrt{40} \approx \mathbf{6.32\text{ cm}}.$$

Template 4: Median of Grouped Frequency Distribution (3 Marks — Q42)

Problem (Real Exam Question):

The distribution below gives the marks/weights of 30 students. Find the median:

Class Interval $40 - 45$ $45 - 50$ $50 - 55$ $55 - 60$ $60 - 65$ $65 - 70$ $70 - 75$
Frequency ($f$) $2$ $3$ $8$ $6$ $6$ $3$ $2$

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Prepare the Cumulative Frequency Table:
Class Interval Frequency ($f$) Cumulative Frequency ($cf$)
$40 - 45$ $2$ $2$
$45 - 50$ $3$ $2 + 3 = 5$
$50 - 55$ $8$ $5 + 8 = 13$
$55 - 60$ (Median Class) $6$ ($f$) $13 + 6 = 19$
$60 - 65$ $6$ $19 + 6 = 25$
$65 - 70$ $3$ $25 + 3 = 28$
$70 - 75$ $2$ $28 + 2 = 30$
Total $N = 30$

  1. Locate the Median Class: $$\frac{N}{2} = \frac{30}{2} = 15$$ The cumulative frequency just greater than $15$ is $19$, which corresponds to the class interval $55 - 60$. Therefore, Median Class $= 55 - 60$.

  2. Identify the Formula Values:

  3. Lower limit of median class, $L = 55$
  4. Class size, $h = 60 - 55 = 5$
  5. Frequency of median class, $f = 6$

  6. Cumulative frequency of preceding class, $cf = 13$

  7. Apply Median Formula: $$\text{Median} = L + \left(\frac{\frac{N}{2} - cf}{f}\right) \times h$$ $$\text{Median} = 55 + \left(\frac{15 - 13}{6}\right) \times 5 = 55 + \left(\frac{2}{6}\right) \times 5 = 55 + \frac{5}{3} = 55 + 1.67 = \mathbf{56.67}$$

Template 5: Coordinate Geometry Ratio & Section Formula (3 Marks — Q40)

Problem (Real Exam Question from 2025 Paper):

Find the ratio in which the y-axis divides the line segment joining the points $A(4, -5)$ and $B(-1, 3)$. Also find the coordinates of the point of intersection.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Set up the variables:
  2. Let the y-axis divide the segment $AB$ in the ratio $k : 1$ at point $P$.

  3. Any point on the y-axis has its x-coordinate equal to $0$. So, let $P = (0, y)$.

  4. Apply Section Formula for the x-coordinate: $$x = \frac{k(x_2) + 1(x_1)}{k + 1}$$ Here $x_1 = 4, \; x_2 = -1, \; x = 0$: $$0 = \frac{k(-1) + 1(4)}{k + 1}$$ $$0 = -k + 4 \implies k = 4$$ Therefore, the required ratio is $k : 1 = \mathbf{4 : 1}$.

  5. Find the y-coordinate of $P$: $$y = \frac{k(y_2) + 1(y_1)}{k + 1} = \frac{4(3) + 1(-5)}{4 + 1} = \frac{12 - 5}{5} = \frac{7}{5} = 1.4$$

  6. Conclusion:

  7. The ratio is $4 : 1$.
  8. The coordinates of the point of intersection are $\mathbf{\left(0, \frac{7}{5}\right)}$.

Template 6: Circle Tangents Calculation (2 Marks — Q33)

Problem:

From an external point $P$, two tangents $PM$ and $PN$ are drawn to a circle of radius $7\text{ cm}$. If the distance of $P$ from the centre $O$ is $25\text{ cm}$, find the length of each tangent.

Step-by-Step Solution:

  1. Since the tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact, $\Delta OMP$ is a right-angled triangle with $\angle OMP = 90^\circ$.
  2. By Pythagoras Theorem in right $\Delta OMP$: $$OP^2 = OM^2 + PM^2$$ $$25^2 = 7^2 + PM^2$$ $$625 = 49 + PM^2 \implies PM^2 = 625 - 49 = 576$$ $$PM = \sqrt{576} = \mathbf{24\text{ cm}}$$
  3. Since tangents from an external point are equal in length: $$PN = PM = \mathbf{24\text{ cm}}.$$
